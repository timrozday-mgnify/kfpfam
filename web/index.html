A weak or unreported candidate may still be true, so precision is estimated from decoys: 1 − decoy / real, per bin, not from hmmsearch's verdict. On the real metagenome, reported hits come from all 50,000 reads hmmsearch rated and their 50,000 reversed copies; the "not reported" bin needs a scan over every model, so it comes from 300 of those reads.

Rejecting promiscuous k-mers (k4 T7, per-read pseudo-alignment)

Most hits are noise, so dropping the k-mers that make the most hits could save time, if each true pair keeps another seed. A pair survives when any seed on a diagonal at the bar is not rejected. Measured by filter search on 50,000 reads of ERR10889147 and the same reads reversed, against hmmsearch. Rules from the index and background need no reads; the sample and repeat rules run at query time. Independent of the policy above.

"Speed, seeding" is 1 ÷ (1 − hits removed), for the seeding stage only. Bray-Curtis compares GA hits per family with no rejection; it shows whether rejection shifts a profile, not only how much recall it costs.

Whole-sample sketch: one k-mer set per sample, no read structure

Instead of aligning each read, all of a sample's k-mers go into one set, and a model is kept for hmmsearch or nail when the set holds a k-mer scoring at or above the bar against one of its windows. Independent of the policy above. Measured with filter sketch on ERR10889147; the bound lumps read-level diagonal or gapped scores per model, which no k-mer set can beat.

Lumping multiplies the search space: every sample residue against every column of a model, about 230 at 50,000 reads and 234 at 1 M. A chance match reaches about log₂ of that at any k, so a true hit has to beat it with one k-mer, and a family seen in few reads is no louder than a family seen in many. Longer k catches more of a hit's score; it does not lower the noise.
